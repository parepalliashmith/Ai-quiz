Essential general knowledge topics for competitive exams

A subject-by-subject map of what to study for GK and general awareness.

General Knowledge (GK) and General Awareness carry heavy weight in almost every competitive exam. The subject is vast, so the key is to focus on high-yield areas and revise them often. Here is a practical breakdown of the topics that matter most and how to approach each.

Indian Polity and Constitution

One of the highest-scoring areas. Focus on the making of the Constitution, the Preamble, Fundamental Rights and Duties, Directive Principles, the structure of Parliament and the judiciary, the President and Prime Minister, and key constitutional amendments. Polity questions are fact-based and reward careful revision.

History

Cover ancient, medieval and modern India, with extra attention to the freedom struggle — important movements, leaders, sessions of the Congress, and key dates. For world history, know major revolutions and world wars at a summary level. Timelines and cause-and-effect are easier to remember than isolated facts.

Geography

Study Indian physical geography (rivers, mountains, climate, soils), economic geography (crops, minerals, industries) and basic world geography (continents, oceans, important lines and climate zones). Maps make this subject far easier — practise locating features rather than memorising lists.

General Science

Expect everyday-application questions from physics, chemistry and biology — units and measurements, the human body, diseases, nutrition, common reactions, and basic laws. You do not need deep theory; you need clear fundamentals and the ability to apply them.

Economy

Understand basic concepts: GDP, inflation, banking and the RBI, budget and taxation, and major government schemes. Pair static economy with current affairs, since exams often ask about recent economic news and policy.

Current Affairs

The most dynamic section. Follow national and international events, awards, sports, appointments, summits and important government initiatives from roughly the last 6–12 months. A few minutes of daily reading, plus a weekly self-test, keeps current affairs fresh without last-minute panic.

How to study GK effectively

  • Pick one subject at a time and finish its high-yield topics before moving on.
  • Make short, revisable notes — one line per fact, not long paragraphs.
  • Test yourself frequently; recalling a fact fixes it far better than re-reading.
  • Revise on a spaced schedule so older topics stay sharp.
