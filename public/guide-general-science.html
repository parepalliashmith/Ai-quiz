General Science basics for exams

The everyday physics, chemistry and biology that exams love.

General Science questions in competitive exams test fundamentals and their everyday applications, not deep theory. These notes highlight the most frequently asked ideas across the three subjects.

Physics

Know the basic units and quantities (the SI unit of force is the newton, of energy the joule, of power the watt, and of electric current the ampere). Understand the laws of motion, work-energy, simple machines, and the basics of light (reflection, refraction) and sound. Everyday applications — why the sky is blue, how a lever reduces effort — are common favourites.

Chemistry

Be comfortable with the structure of the atom, the periodic table, and common acids, bases and salts (and the pH scale). Know everyday chemistry: the chemical names of common substances (common salt is sodium chloride, baking soda is sodium bicarbonate), the difference between physical and chemical changes, and the gases involved in respiration and combustion.

Biology

Focus on the human body and health: the systems (digestive, respiratory, circulatory, nervous), important vitamins and the deficiency diseases they cause, and common communicable and non-communicable diseases. Also cover the cell as the basic unit of life, photosynthesis in plants, and basic classification of living things.

How to study science for exams

  • Prioritise everyday applications over heavy derivations.
  • Make a one-line note for each fact — "SI unit of power = watt".
  • Revise with frequent short quizzes rather than long reading sessions.
