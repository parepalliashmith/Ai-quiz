Geography essentials for exams

Indian physical geography and world basics, made simple.

Geography becomes easy once you connect it to a map. Instead of memorising lists, visualise where things are. These notes summarise the high-yield areas for competitive exams.

Physical features of India

India has four broad physical divisions: the Himalayan mountains in the north, the Northern Plains formed by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems, the Peninsular Plateau in the south, and the coastal plains with islands. The Himalayas are young fold mountains and the source of many perennial rivers.

Rivers

Indian rivers are grouped into Himalayan rivers (perennial, fed by snow and rain — the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems) and Peninsular rivers (mostly rain-fed — the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri and Narmada). The Godavari is the longest river of peninsular India. The Narmada and Tapi are notable for flowing westward into the Arabian Sea.

Climate and monsoon

India has a tropical monsoon climate. The south-west monsoon (June to September) brings most of the annual rainfall, while the north-east monsoon brings rain to the south-eastern coast in winter. The monsoon is central to Indian agriculture and the economy.

Soils and agriculture

Major soil types include alluvial soil (the most fertile, found in the plains), black soil (ideal for cotton), red soil, and laterite soil. Knowing which crop suits which soil and climate — rice in high-rainfall areas, wheat in the plains, cotton in black soil — answers many exam questions.

World geography basics

Be comfortable with the seven continents and five oceans, major latitudes (the Equator, Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn), time zones and the International Date Line, and important climate zones. A world map on your wall does more than a dozen lists.
