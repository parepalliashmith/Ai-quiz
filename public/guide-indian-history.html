Indian History — important points for exams

A compact revision of ancient, medieval and modern India.

History carries a steady share of marks in competitive exams, and the facts rarely change, so good notes pay off year after year. Focus on timelines and cause-and-effect rather than isolated dates — they are far easier to remember.

Ancient India

The Indus Valley Civilisation (around 2500 BCE) was known for planned cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, advanced drainage and standardised weights. The Vedic period that followed gave us the four Vedas. Later, the Mauryan Empire rose under Chandragupta Maurya, guided by Chanakya (Kautilya), and reached its height under Ashoka, who embraced Buddhism after the Kalinga war. The Gupta period is often called the "Golden Age" of India for its achievements in mathematics, astronomy and art.

Medieval India

The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) saw five dynasties, beginning with the Slave dynasty founded by Qutb-ud-din Aibak. The Mughal Empire was established by Babur after the First Battle of Panipat in 1526 and flourished under Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan, who built the Taj Mahal. The Vijayanagara and Bahmani kingdoms were major powers in the south, and the Marathas later rose under Shivaji.

Modern India and the freedom struggle

The British East India Company gained a foothold after the Battle of Plassey (1757) and the Battle of Buxar (1764). The Revolt of 1857 was the first large-scale uprising against British rule. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. The freedom movement gathered pace with Mahatma Gandhi's leadership through the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920), the Civil Disobedience Movement and the Dandi Salt March (1930), and the Quit India Movement (1942). India gained independence on 15 August 1947.

How to remember it all

Group events into themes and movements rather than memorising stray dates, and revise with frequent self-testing. Type "Indian History" or a specific period on the AIQUIZ home page to generate a quick quiz and check what you remember.
