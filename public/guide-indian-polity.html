Indian Polity — key notes for exams

High-yield revision points on the Constitution and Indian government.

Indian Polity is one of the most scoring sections in almost every competitive exam, because the questions are factual and the syllabus is finite. These notes cover the areas that appear most often. Use them for a quick revision, then test yourself with a quiz.

The Constitution and the Preamble

The Constitution of India was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar chaired the drafting committee and is regarded as its chief architect. The Preamble declares India to be a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic and sets out the objectives of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. The words "socialist", "secular" and "integrity" were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976).

Fundamental Rights

Part III of the Constitution guarantees six Fundamental Rights: the Right to Equality (Articles 14–18), the Right to Freedom (19–22), the Right against Exploitation (23–24), the Right to Freedom of Religion (25–28), Cultural and Educational Rights (29–30), and the Right to Constitutional Remedies (32). Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution because it lets citizens approach the Supreme Court directly to enforce their rights. The Right to Property was removed as a Fundamental Right by the 44th Amendment and is now a legal right under Article 300A.

Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties

The Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) are guidelines for the government to establish a just society. They are not enforceable in court but are fundamental in governance. The Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A, Article 51A) were added by the 42nd Amendment on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee; there are now eleven duties of every citizen.

Parliament and the Executive

The Union Parliament has two houses: the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). The President is the constitutional head of the Union, while real executive power rests with the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers, who are collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Money bills can originate only in the Lok Sabha.

The Judiciary

The Supreme Court is the apex court and the guardian of the Constitution, with powers of judicial review. High Courts stand at the head of the judiciary in each state. The judiciary's independence is protected through secure tenure and fixed service conditions for judges.
