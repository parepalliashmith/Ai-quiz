Time management and exam-day strategy

Study smarter during preparation, and attempt the paper smarter on the day.

Two students can know the same syllabus and score very differently — the difference is often time management. This guide covers how to use your time well while preparing, and how to handle the clock inside the exam hall.

Build a daily routine you can keep

Fix a regular start time and study in focused blocks rather than long, distracted stretches. A simple structure works well: a morning block for your hardest subject when your mind is fresh, a shorter afternoon block for practice, and an evening block for revising what you learned earlier in the day. Protect this routine; consistency over months beats occasional long sessions.

Use the Pomodoro method for focus

Study in 25-minute focused sprints followed by a 5-minute break, and take a longer break after four sprints. Phones away, one task per sprint. This keeps concentration high and prevents burnout. If 25 minutes feels short, extend to 40–50 minute blocks — the principle is the same: deep focus, then a real break.

Balance learning, practice and revision

Many aspirants spend all their time learning new material and never revise, then forget most of it. A healthy split is roughly 40% new learning, 30% practice and self-testing, and 30% revision. As the exam nears, shift more time toward revision and mock tests.

Plan the paper before you start writing

In the first minute of the exam, glance through the paper and decide an order of attempt. Start with the sections and questions you are confident about to bank easy marks and build momentum. Leave tough or time-consuming questions for a second pass so one hard question never eats the time meant for ten easy ones.

Handle negative marking wisely

If there is negative marking, do not guess blindly. Attempt questions you are sure of, make an educated guess only when you can eliminate two options, and skip the rest. One silly negative mark can cost you the marks of several correct answers. Practising mock tests with negative marking trains this instinct — AIQUIZ's Exam Mode adds negative marking so your practice matches the real paper.

Watch the clock, section by section

Divide the total time across sections before you begin and keep rough checkpoints — for example, "I should be done with Section A by the 30-minute mark." If a question is taking too long, mark it and move on; you can return if time allows. Finishing every section is almost always better than perfecting one and abandoning another.

Mind and body on exam day

Sleep well the night before — a rested brain recalls faster than a tired one that studied all night. Eat a light meal, reach the centre early, and read all instructions carefully. Stay calm if a question stumps you; move on and come back. Confidence and composure genuinely add marks.
